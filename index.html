What we’ve sent so far.

Five machines that changed what we know, from a Moon landing to a spacecraft flying through the Sun’s atmosphere.

1977

Voyager 1

The most distant human-made object. It crossed into interstellar space in 2012, and its Golden Record carries sounds and images of Earth.

In interstellar space since 2012

2021

James Webb Space Telescope

A 6.5-metre mirror of 18 gold-coated hexagons, 1.5 million km from Earth, reading infrared light from some of the first galaxies.

1.5 million km from Earth

1969

Apollo 11

On 20 July, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon for about two and a half hours while Michael Collins orbited above.

20 July 1969

2018

Parker Solar Probe

The first spacecraft to fly through the Sun’s corona. At its closest it moves at about 692,000 km/h, the fastest any human-made object has travelled.

692,000 km/h

2021

Perseverance

Landed in Mars’s Jezero Crater and has been collecting sealed rock samples from an ancient river delta. Its helicopter, Ingenuity, flew 72 times.

Landed 18 February 2021

The farther you look, the older the light.

Light covers about 300,000 km every second. Distances in space are easiest to feel as the time light needs to cross them.

  1. 8.3minutes

    Sunlight reaching Earth. You always see the Sun as it was eight minutes ago.

  2. 4.2years

    Light from Proxima Centauri, the nearest star after the Sun.

  3. 100,000years

    Light crossing the Milky Way from one edge to the other.

  4. 2.5million years

    Light from the Andromeda galaxy, which you can see with your own eyes on a dark night.

  5. 13.8billion years

    The age of the universe. The oldest light we can see has travelled almost this long.
